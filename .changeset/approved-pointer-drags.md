After you approve the input queue, complete web drags that have no background route can use your pointer, one at a time. While approval lasts, a thin red edge runs around your screens, following a MacBook display's rounded corners and notch, and a small bar at the bottom of the screen shows what is happening, how long approval has lasted, and a Stop button. Move your mouse or press a key to take control back. A stopped drag releases its button and reports that it stopped. When approval ends normally, your pointer and front app come back.

Background input stays first and needs no approval. Split presses held across calls and real-keyboard actions are not executed by this queue.
